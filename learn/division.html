Learning path · Division

Share it. Group it. Check it.

Division can ask how many belong in each equal group, or how many equal groups you can make. Try both stories with objects before moving to written questions.

Before you begin

Start with a collection you can count

The learner should be able to count a small collection and compare whether groups have the same amount. Try sharing six counters into two groups. If a group has an extra counter, move it and talk about “equal”.

Known multiplication facts help with written division, but objects can come first. For long division, add a check that tens and ones, subtraction and single-digit multiplication are familiar.

Worked example

Worked example: two ways to see 12 ÷ 3 = 4

Sharing: how many in each group?

Draw three circles. Share 12 counters between the 3 circles, one counter at a time, until none remain. Each circle has 4.

Here, 3 tells you the number of groups and 4 tells you the amount in each. Check that every circle has the same amount.

Grouping: how many groups?

Start again with 12 counters. Put them into groups of 3. You can make 4 groups, with nothing left over.

This time, 3 tells you the amount in a group and 4 tells you the number of groups. Check either story using 3 × 4 = 12.

Keep the story attached to the answer: four counters per circle is different from four groups. Both illustrate 12 ÷ 3, but the unit tells you what the result means.

Practice plan

A short session without a countdown

  1. Show: make the two 12 ÷ 3 arrangements above. Ask the learner to tell you what the 3 means in each one.
  2. Connect: find 3 × 4 on the times-table chart. Write 12 ÷ 3 = 4 and 12 ÷ 4 = 3 beside it.
  3. Try: choose a familiar divisor in Division Practice. Try three typed answers. These are exact divisions with no remainders.
  4. Check: multiply each answer by the divisor. If the starting number returns, the calculation checks out. Pause for an offscreen sorting or sharing activity.

One careful explanation is more useful here than racing through a whole set. Around five minutes can be enough; repeat with a smaller collection if needed.

Next step

Keep the next step clear

Exact division first

In 12 ÷ 3, every counter is used. The printable exact-division sheet deliberately uses only positive whole-number answers. It does not include division by zero, fractions or remainder questions.

Then discuss what is left

With 14 counters in groups of 3, make four full groups and leave two counters. Write 14 ÷ 3 = 4 remainder 2. Check: (3 × 4) + 2 = 14. The remainder must be smaller than the divisor.

Written methods and games
