Clocks

Read an analogue clock

Start here if: the learner recognises numbers 1–12. Begin with the short hour hand; counting in fives can come later.

Try together

Explain why the short hand sits between 3 and 4 at 3:30, rather than jumping from number to number.

Division

Share equally, then divide

Start here if: the learner can count a small collection and put objects into equal groups. A few multiplication facts help with the next step.

Try together

Show both “12 shared between 3” and “how many groups of 3 fit in 12?”

Spelling

Look closely at a word

Start here if: the learner can say a familiar word and identify some of its sounds or letters. Choose a word they understand.

Try together

Say “ship”, notice that sh is two letters representing one sound, then cover and write it.

Any skill, any day

A small routine you can reuse

  1. Notice

    Ask one question before opening a tool: “What do you already know about this?” Listen for the method, not just the answer.

  2. Try

    Explore one example together, then try two or three without a timer. Leave time to draw, count, say the idea aloud or change a guess.

  3. Explain and pause

    Ask “How could we check?” Pick one thing to revisit next time, then take an offscreen break. The plan is a suggestion, not a daily requirement.
