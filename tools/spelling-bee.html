Why Spelling Practice Matters

Spelling is more than memorising letter sequences — it builds phonemic awareness, vocabulary and reading fluency. Children who spell well tend to read more confidently and write more freely.

This tool combines listening, a written hint, and typing. Try the spelling learning path for a sequence that also includes saying and writing words away from the screen.

Choosing the Right Level

Easy uses simple 3–4 letter CVC words (cat, dog, sun) — perfect for early readers aged 4–6. Medium introduces common words with blends, digraphs and silent letters for ages 6–8. Hard tackles multi-syllable words like “beautiful” and “necessary” for ages 8–11.

Start with comfortable words and choose another level when ready. The streak counter is feedback, not a required target. Make a paper spelling sheet if writing a few words together would be useful.

Tips for Better Spelling

Encourage children to say the word out loud before typing it. Breaking words into syllables ("but-ter-fly") makes long words less intimidating. After getting a word wrong, look at the correct spelling together and spot the tricky part.

Pair this tool with reading — children who read for pleasure naturally encounter correct spellings in context, reinforcing what they practise here.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does this spelling bee tool work?

Press Hear the Word to listen to the word, which also plays automatically. Read the hint, type the spelling into the box and press Check Spelling. The tool tracks your correct answers and streak. Choose Easy, Medium or Hard to match your child's level.

What age is this spelling tool suitable for?

Ages are a rough guide, not an assessment. Easy is aimed at about ages 4–6 with short words such as cat, dog and sun. Medium is aimed at about ages 6–8 with common words that include blends and silent letters. Hard is aimed at about ages 8–11 with longer words such as 'beautiful' and 'necessary'.

Can I use this spelling bee on a phone or tablet?

Yes, if the browser supports speech. Words are read aloud using your browser's built-in speech synthesis, and support varies by device. Without speech the tool can't play the words and shows a message, so try Chrome or Edge instead.
