How to Do Long Division Step by Step

Long division breaks big division problems into a series of smaller, manageable steps: Divide, Multiply, Subtract, Bring Down. By repeating this cycle for each digit of the dividend, children can solve problems that would otherwise feel overwhelming.

  1. Divide. Look at the first digit of the dividend. Determine how many times the divisor fits into that digit. Write the result above the dividend as the first digit of the quotient.
  2. Multiply. Multiply the quotient digit you just wrote by the divisor. Write the product below the digit(s) of the dividend you used.
  3. Subtract. Subtract the product from the digit(s) above it to find the difference.
  4. Bring Down. Bring down the next digit of the dividend and write it next to the difference. This forms a new number.
  5. Repeat. Repeat the Divide–Multiply–Subtract–Bring Down cycle with the new number. Continue until there are no more digits to bring down. Any remaining value is the remainder.

Tips for Teaching Long Division to Kids

This tool uses small divisors. If the meaning of division is still new, begin with sharing and grouping in our division learning path before working through the written method. The simple division guide for parents covers the ideas behind it.

Encourage children to say each step out loud: "How many times does 3 go into 7?" Verbalising the thinking helps cement the method and makes it easier to spot mistakes early.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you do long division step by step?

Long division repeats four steps: Divide, Multiply, Subtract, Bring Down. Divide the first digit (or first two digits) of the dividend by the divisor to find the first quotient digit. Multiply that digit by the divisor and subtract the result. Bring down the next digit and repeat until no digits are left. Any amount left over is the remainder.

What is the bus stop method in division?

The bus stop method is a UK name for writing division in a bracket layout. The dividend sits inside the bracket, the divisor sits outside it on the left, and the quotient is written on top, digit by digit. This tool uses that layout.

When do children learn long division?

Timing varies by school and curriculum. Many children meet it between ages 8 and 11. They usually need secure multiplication and division facts first. This tool uses divisors from 2 to 5 and two-digit quotients with remainders, so it suits early practice with the method.
