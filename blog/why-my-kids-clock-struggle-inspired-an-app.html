Why My Kid's Struggle with Clocks Inspired Me to Build an App

The true story behind Clock Master — how one mom's frustration led to building an educational game.

It was a Tuesday evening. Homework time. My son stared at the analog clock on his worksheet with a look I knew all too well — a mix of confusion and frustration that was about to turn into tears.

"Mama, I don't understand. Why does the little hand mean hours and the big hand means minutes? That doesn't make sense!"

He had a point. To a 5-year-old, it really doesn't make intuitive sense. And honestly? In that moment, I struggled to explain it in a way that clicked. We'd been at it for 20 minutes, and we were both exhausted.

The Search for Help

That night, after he went to bed, I searched for an app that would help us practise reading clocks.

What I found was... disappointing:

  • App #1: Beautiful design, but wanted $9.99/month. For a clock app. For a five-year-old.
  • App #2: Free, but bombarded with full-screen ads every 30 seconds. My son clicked on three ads before I could take the phone back.
  • App #3: Actually decent content, but the UI was so dated it looked like it was built in 2010. My son refused to use it.
  • App #4: Required an account with email, birthday, and school name. Absolutely not.

Not a single app hit all three basics: free, safe, and actually fun.

The Ridiculous Idea

I'm not a developer. I'm a mom. But I had a background in product management and a stubbornness that my husband describes as "concerning." So I thought: What if I just... build one?

It started as a toy project — learn some React, make a simple clock with draggable hands, add some colorful buttons. Let my son test it.

"Mama, can I play the clock game again?" — my son, at breakfast, voluntarily asking to practice telling time.

That sentence changed how I thought about our practice time. He was asking to play. That was encouraging for our family, not proof that a game would work the same way for every child.

From Side Project to Clock Master

Over the next few months, the app grew. I added:

  • Guided lessons — now 11 clock chapters, including the hands, reading time, time arithmetic, and clock angles
  • A racing game — answer clock questions to boost your car. My son's competitive streak kicked in.
  • Space exploration — fly a rocket to 30 space destinations by solving time problems and read facts along the way.
  • Otterly and friends — animated companions and voice guidance in the current Clock Master game.
  • A parental gate — because I know what it's like to have a child accidentally change all the settings.

The games need no account and keep progress locally. Today you can play the full browser games with browser-only saves and no native rewarded videos, or choose Android for offline play. Android can show AdMob banners and optional rewarded ads when online. Hosting and other services still process some information, as our privacy policy explains. There are no in-app purchases.

What I Learned Along the Way

Building Clock Master taught me something broader about how kids learn:

  1. Context can invite practice. Clock questions gave the race a purpose. That helped us begin, but I did not measure a learning gain against worksheets.
  2. Notice more than rewards. Coins and car colours are enjoyable; asking how he read the clock tells me more about what he understands.
  3. Guidance needs options. Voice instructions can help, and an adult can still pause to explain or choose a quieter activity.
  4. Keep the format flexible. A game, a paper clock, and a conversation each have a place. No one format removes every difficulty.

And Then Came Math Tank

A few months later, multiplication tables entered our lives. Same struggle, same tears, same search for a good app. But this time, I didn't search. I built.

Math Tank applied the same interest in playful practice to multiplication and division, with guided lessons, untimed practice, and timed tank battles. Families can choose a challenge rather than treating the game as a replacement for every other way of learning.

The Otterly Games Philosophy

Every feature in our apps passes one test: "Would I want this for my own child?" If the answer is no, it doesn't ship. That's it. That's the strategy.

What's Next

Otterly Games is just getting started. I'm one mom with a laptop, some AI tools, and a deep understanding of what parents actually need. If these games help even one other parent avoid a tearful homework session, it will all have been worth it.
