Choosing Math Apps for Kids — A Parent's Shortlist

A practical checklist, our own Math Tank, and other options to explore. Not an independent benchmark.

A useful math app should fit the skill your child is working on, your budget, and the way your family likes to practise. Start with a specific need rather than a "best app" badge.

Ownership disclosure: We make Math Tank and Clock Master. This is our product perspective, not an independent ranking. The other apps below are starting points to explore, not winners from a controlled comparison.

How to use this shortlist: We look at the learning task, opportunities to think through answers, adult controls, accounts, and costs. We have not tested every current version or measured learning outcomes across these apps. Availability, pricing, ads, and privacy settings can change; check each provider's current information before installing. This is not a new annual benchmark.

What Makes a Good Math App?

Before the list, here's my criteria. A good math app should:

  • Teach concepts, not just drill facts — understanding WHY 6×7=42 matters more than memorizing it
  • Make stopping easy — clear round endings and room for breaks
  • Explain privacy clearly — check what the app and its providers process, rather than relying on a safety badge
  • Have reasonable monetization — either free, one-time purchase, or affordable subscription
  • Offer a suitable challenge — selectable levels and untimed practice can help; they are not the same as automatic adaptation

Other Apps to Explore

2. Khan Academy Kids

Ages: 2-8 · Focus: All subjects · Platform: iOS, Android

Khan Academy Kids combines early learning activities and stories across subjects. Explore its counting and early arithmetic activities if you want broader practice rather than a single-skill game.

Check before choosing: Read the provider's current description, age guidance, device requirements, and account and privacy information.

3. Prodigy Math

Ages: 6-12 · Focus: Curriculum-aligned math · Platform: iOS, Android, Web

Prodigy wraps math practice in a wizard adventure. It may suit a child who enjoys an ongoing game world, but a lively game is not evidence that it will fit every learner.

Check before choosing: Compare the current free and membership features, account requirements, and family controls on Prodigy's website. We do not quote a subscription price because plans can change.

4. Moose Math (Duck Duck Moose)

Ages: 3-7 · Focus: Counting, addition, geometry · Platform: iOS, Android

Moose Math is an early-math option with playful counting and arithmetic activities. Look at the tasks with your child to see whether they match what they are ready to practise.

Check before choosing: Find the current app through Duck Duck Moose and check its store listing for availability, cost, and privacy details. It is not labelled here as a paid app.

5. Todo Math

Ages: 3-8 · Focus: Comprehensive math · Platform: iOS, Android

Todo Math offers a range of early math activities. Consider it when you want to explore several skills rather than focus only on multiplication facts.

Check before choosing: Review the current activities, free access, subscription terms, and accessibility options on Todo Math's website. A daily goal is optional family structure, not a guarantee of progress.

6. DragonBox Numbers

Ages: 4-8 · Focus: Number sense · Platform: iOS, Android

DragonBox Numbers uses number characters called Nooms to explore combining, splitting, and comparing quantities. It is an option to explore for number sense, rather than a direct replacement for times-table practice.

Check before choosing: Review the current purchase or subscription options and device support through DragonBox. We have not measured its effectiveness against other approaches.

Red Flags to Watch For

When evaluating math apps, avoid ones that:

  • Show ads every 30 seconds — especially full-screen video ads. Kids tap on them accidentally and end up in the App Store
  • Require personal information — a math app has no business knowing your child's school name, birthday, or email
  • Make costs unclear — check what is included, trial terms, and how an adult can cancel
  • Offer rewards for watching ads — decide whether this fits your family; our Android apps offer optional rewarded videos, while our browser versions do not
  • Give little useful feedback — look for explanations or a chance to revisit mistakes, not just points

The Bottom Line

The best math app is the one your child will actually use. Try a few from this list, see which one clicks, and let them run with it. And remember: apps are a supplement, not a replacement. Real-world math — cooking, shopping, building — is still the most powerful teacher of all.
